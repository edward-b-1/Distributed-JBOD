Device availability on the Objects page

The Objects page should say how many devices are unavailable and what that means for the objects listed. The meaning depends on the scheme's k and m and on the count U of unavailable devices, in the five cases below. Every mock uses the demo cluster as it stands: scheme 2+1, five devices in use, two of them on the unreachable devbox4.

The five cases, and what the page can honestly say

Cases 2 and 3 are one row here: the count alone cannot tell whether the missing shards were data or parity, so the sentence covers both without claiming either. Whether the listing is complete comes from the node, which since #218 reports the devices it could not read and whether any object could be hidden entirely.

CaseConditionMarkWhat the strip says
1U = 0dotEvery object can be read at full speed; any m devices can fail without loss.
2, 30 < U ≤ mtriangleEvery object can still be read. Objects with a shard on the unavailable devices are rebuilt from their other shards, slower when the missing shard held data. One more failure may make some objects unreadable.
4m < U < k+msquareObjects with more than m shards on the unavailable devices cannot be read; the record view says which. The listing is complete.
5U ≥ k+msquareAs 4, and objects stored only on the unavailable devices are missing from the listing, which is incomplete.

A. A status line above the table

One row between the filters and the key table, the width of the table: the mark and headline, the consequence in muted text, and the node count at the right. Recommended: it is read before the list, it fits the Objects page's existing rows, and it has room for the sentence that matters.

C. A banner across the page

The transport banners' treatment: a filled strip across the top of the Objects page, amber or red, only when U > 0. Loudest, and nothing at all in case 1, which loses the reassurance that everything is available. It also reads as a page-wide warning although it is specific to the listing below.

Recommendation: A. It is the only treatment with room for the consequence, which is the point, and it is present in case 1 as well, so an operator learns to look there. Every input is already on the page: k and m from the document, each device's availability from the status, and the listing's own completeness from the node since #218. It is a UI-only change.

The device count is of devices in use, active and draining, since a removed device holds nothing. The nodes figure is the muted text at the right, because a node matters here only through its devices.
